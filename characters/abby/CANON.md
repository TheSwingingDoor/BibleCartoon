# Abby — canonical rulings

## Identity

- Name: Abby.
- Adult female anthropomorphic West Highland White Terrier.
- Owner and working bartender of The Swinging Door.
- Recurring supporting character; she appears selectively and has high narrative authority.
- Canon version: 1.0.0.

## Immutable physical traits

- White Westie head fur with visibly layered, directional texture.
- Upright triangular ears with darker inner-ear wash.
- Short, softly squared canine muzzle and a solid black canine nose.
- Expressive human-style eyes integrated into the canine face.
- Feminine hourglass torso with fuller bust and narrow waist.
- Slim, smooth, hairless, shapely legs with a natural thigh gap.
- Five-digit anthropomorphic hands when fingers are visible.
- No tail in any view, pose, outfit, or scene.
- Attractive adult presentation; never childlike, puppy-like, or infantilized.

## Immutable work outfit

- Fitted light collared blouse.
- Sleeves rolled neatly to the forearm.
- Top button open.
- Modest scalloped lace inset visible at the neckline.
- Very short dark fitted bartender skirt/apron.
- Large functional bow tied at the center back.
- Folded bar towel over one shoulder.
- Close-fitting single strand of small round pearls.
- One centered oval faceted gemstone in a simple dark metal bezel.
- One delicate bracelet.
- Black closed-toe mid-height heels.

## Immutable role behavior

- Abby behaves like the owner of the room, not hired decoration.
- She is competent at bar work and handles props naturally.
- Her default public expression is a welcoming, intelligent smile.
- Her wit is concise; she does not chatter to prove she is smart.
- She may be dry, skeptical, firm, or gently condescending, but never cruel.
- In ordinary scenes she works behind the bar. She may leave that position for an explicit owner action: opening or closing, greeting at the swinging doors, checking the room, or handling a practical task.

## Controlled variation

These may change when the scene requires it:

- Gaze direction, eyelid compression, brow angle, mouth shape, and ear attention.
- Which shoulder carries the towel, if continuity within the scene is maintained.
- Bracelet wrist, only if a production-specific continuity decision is recorded; otherwise keep it on Abby's right wrist.
- Skirt/apron surface pattern may simplify at small scale but remains dark and fitted.
- Props held, hand gestures, and weight distribution.
- Crop, viewpoint, lighting wash, and amount of environment.

## Forbidden drift

- Tail, tail tuft, tail opening, or implied tail bulge.
- Furry legs, paws in place of heels, stockings, boots, or bare feet.
- Long skirt, pants, shorts, full dress, vest, bow tie, or bartender costume substitution.
- Plain dark choker replacing the pearl collar and oval gemstone.
- Missing lace, missing bracelet, missing towel, or fully buttoned blouse in a normal work scene.
- Giant cartoon eyes, dot eyes, all-black eyes, empty irises, animal-only bead eyes, or photoreal human eyes pasted onto fur.
- Human face, human nose, human ears, hair hairstyle, or exposed human skin.
- Childlike proportions, puppy behavior, vacant glamour pose, or exaggerated pin-up anatomy.
- Cruel, ditzy, submissive, naïve, sloppy, intoxicated, or incompetent characterization.
- Color, glossy 3D, anime, photorealism, vector-flat art, or dense cross-hatching.
- Placing Abby as a casual patron when she is meant to be working.

## Canon conflict rule

If a generated image looks appealing but breaks one locked rule, the image is wrong. Do not rewrite canon to excuse an accident. Only an explicit owner-approved design change can alter a locked rule.
