# Visual Construction Specification

This document explains how to reconstruct Drew without redesigning him. The master model remains the highest visual authority.

## Overall silhouette

Drew has a vertical, elegant silhouette built from three readable masses:

1. a compact flamingo head and beak;
2. a long, slim S-curved neck;
3. an average anthropomorphic torso with long bird legs and feathered wing-arms.

The silhouette must remain recognizable at thumbnail size. The neck curve and downturned beak are the strongest identifiers; the bow tie is the third.

## Proportion anchors

Use the head height—from crown to lower beak curve—as one visual unit.

| Feature | Target relationship | Reject when |
|---|---|---|
| Total standing height | roughly 7.5–8 head units | childlike, squat, or fashion-model extreme |
| Neck centerline | roughly 2–2.25 head units of visible curved path | short, straight, thick, or swan-heavy |
| Shoulder width | about 1.5 head widths | bodybuilder-wide or pinched |
| Torso | moderate taper from shoulder to hips | hourglass, pot belly, or rigid rectangle |
| Wing-arm reach | feather tips reach upper-thigh region at rest | tiny decorative wings or floor-length wings |
| Legs | long and slim, supporting an upright stance | human calves, short legs, or stilt exaggeration |

These are consistency guides, not drafting measurements. Match the master sheet before matching a number.

## Head

- Small mature flamingo cranium with fine short feathering.
- Crown is gently rounded, never oversized or domed.
- No human hair, ears, nose, lips, moustache, or jaw.
- Head connects cleanly into the neck; avoid a human chin or throat.

## Eyes

Drew's eyes balance life and species accuracy:

- small relative to the head;
- visible white area, but not a large cartoon eyeball;
- distinct medium-dark iris and darker pupil;
- one small catchlight per visible eye;
- fine upper and lower lid contours;
- gaze direction must be readable;
- emotion comes mainly from lid aperture, iris placement, and head pitch.

Do not use all-black bead eyes, concentric alien circles, oversized whites, thick human eyelashes, or photoreal human eyes pasted into a bird face.

## Beak

- Flamingo-specific, deep at the base and strongly downturned.
- Proximal portion is pale; distal portion is dark.
- The dark tip occupies approximately the final third, following the bend.
- Nostrils are small and understated.
- The beak seam is available for speech and smiles but stays anatomically plausible.
- Never add teeth, lips, a duck bill, parrot hook, pelican pouch, or human mouth.

## Neck

- Long, slim, and distinctly flamingo.
- Uses a smooth S-curve rather than a straight tube.
- Curve changes with acting: taller when alert, slightly compressed when skeptical, forward-leaning when curious.
- Thickness tapers subtly toward the head and remains consistent through the curve.
- Fine feather texture follows the neck's length without becoming fur.

## Torso and feather covering

- Average healthy build.
- Entire body is covered in dense, short natural plumage.
- The base sheet is G-rated and anatomically simplified.
- Chest and abdomen use feather direction and gray wash for volume, not human muscle definition.
- Avoid exaggerated pectorals, abdominals, hips, or fashion-model anatomy.

## Wing-arms

Wing-arms replace human arms. They are slim, flexible, and capable of acting.

- Small layered contour feathers at shoulder.
- Directional feathers follow upper arm and elbow bend.
- Longer tapered feathers create the forearm silhouette.
- Wing folds remain compact at rest.
- No separate extra wings behind the arms.
- No smooth skin cylinders or sleeve-like feather texture.

See `ANATOMY-AND-GESTURE.md` for hand construction.

## Legs and feet

- Long, slim bird legs with minimal joint emphasis.
- Feet are flamingo-like and webbed, supporting the stylized upright stance.
- Toes are broad enough to read in ink; avoid human feet, shoes on the base model, raptor talons, or mammal paws.

## Bow tie

- Solid black.
- Symmetrical but hand-drawn, with a centered knot.
- Small enough not to consume the neck.
- Always present, including under or over scene clothing.
- Never patterned, brightly colored, replaced by a necktie, or omitted.

## Line and wash

- Hand-drawn black pen or pencil-ink line with natural variation.
- Controlled crosshatching and short feather strokes.
- Restrained gray wash for depth.
- Warm off-white paper.
- Clean hierarchy: character first, props second, background third.
- No color, glossy digital airbrush, heavy charcoal smearing, flat vector fill, 3D rendering, anime, or photorealism.
