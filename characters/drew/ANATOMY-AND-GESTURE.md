# Wing-Arm, Feather-Hand, and Gesture Guide

Drew's arms are wings adapted for expressive cartoon acting. They must read as bird anatomy first and functional arms second.

## Construction

1. **Shoulder coverts** — small overlapping feathers anchor the wing to the torso.
2. **Upper-wing feathers** — medium feathers follow the upper arm and preserve a slim silhouette.
3. **Elbow transition** — feather direction changes around the bend; the joint is readable without exposed skin.
4. **Forewing feathers** — longer tapered feathers form the lower arm and lead to the feather-hand.
5. **Feather-digits** — one short thumb-feather and two longer finger-feathers articulate at the end.

## Nail treatment

Each feather-digit ends in one tiny pale avian nail point. The nail is no more than roughly one tenth of the visible digit length. It is short, softly curved, and drawn with one or two restrained lines.

Never use human nail beds, cuticles, polish, black tips, long claws, hooked talons, or manicure shapes.

## Approved gestures

| Gesture | Construction | Character read |
|---|---|---|
| Resting | wing folded close; feather-digits relaxed | composed |
| One-digit question | one finger-feather lifted; others folded | precise curiosity |
| Open explanation | feather-digits separated slightly; palm area never exposed | reasoned clarification |
| Martini hold | thumb-feather opposes two finger-feathers around stem | controlled, elegant |
| Bar lean | forewing rests on surface; long feathers follow plane | relaxed confidence |
| Point | one feather-digit extends; arm stays feathered | emphasis, never aggression |
| Shrug | both wings lift slightly from torso | dry uncertainty |
| Listening fold | wing-hands lightly overlap or rest | attention |
| Writing/reading | small feather-digits control pen or paper edge | literate and deliberate |

## Gesture scale

Drew usually acts between 20% and 60% of full extension. Large spread-wing gestures are rare and reserved for genuine surprise or a scene that explicitly requires them. Default acting is economical.

## Holding objects

Do not morph the feather-hand into a human hand to solve a prop. Simplify the grip and object instead. Show contact between the thumb-feather and one or two finger-feathers. Keep the object readable and the anatomy consistent.

## Silhouette test

At small scale, the viewer should see a feathered wing contour and a clear gesture. If the gesture reads only because the hand has become human, it fails.
