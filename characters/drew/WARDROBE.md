# Wardrobe and Clothing Fit

## Permanent rule

Drew's solid black bow tie is always present. It is the only permanent accessory and the only item on the base model.

All other clothing is dictated by the scene. Clothing does not become canon merely because it appears in a generated image.

## Fit rules

- Clothing fits over the canonical torso without changing body width or posture.
- Wing-arms remain feathered and recognizable. Garments may frame or partially cover them, but must not convert them into human sleeves and hands.
- Neck openings accommodate the long S-curved neck.
- The black bow tie sits visibly at the neck, above or integrated with a collar.
- Trousers, if used, fit the long bird legs without inventing human shoes unless footwear is required by the scene.
- Clothing follows the same black-ink and gray-wash rendering style.

## Scene examples

| Scene | Allowed clothing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Default model/reference | bow tie only | never use this presentation to infer scene wardrobe |
| Bar conversation | bow tie only, shirt, vest, jacket, or suit if requested | do not add a suit by habit |
| Business/finance | tailored jacket or suit | mature, restrained; bow tie visible |
| Golf | collared golf shirt, optional cap | wing construction preserved |
| Fishing | practical shirt, vest, optional hat | prop and safety needs dictate fit |
| Travel | jacket, coat, or context-specific attire | no permanent luggage or uniform |
| Formal event | dinner jacket or evening attire | bow tie remains the canonical black tie |
| Outdoor weather | coat, rainwear, or scarf | scarf must not replace or hide bow tie without explicit approval |

## Forbidden wardrobe drift

No permanent suit, random costume, patterned bow tie, jewelry, nail polish, high fashion silhouette, child clothing, brand logos, color accents, or clothing that hides species anatomy without a scene reason.
