# Voice and Behavior

## Voice profile

Drew speaks in precise, economical sentences. He notices incentives, contradictions, market behavior, language, status, and institutional absurdity. He sounds informed without lecturing and witty without chasing a punchline.

## Dialogue characteristics

- Usually one sentence.
- Prefer 6–18 words for a caption line.
- Uses concrete nouns and clean verbs.
- May use financial, political, or business language when the audience will understand it.
- The joke usually turns on one precise observation.
- Questions can be genuinely curious or quietly revealing.
- Understatement is stronger than exclamation.

## What Drew does not sound like

- a ranting pundit;
- a professor delivering a paragraph;
- a naïve child;
- a cruel cynic;
- a frantic comedian;
- a generic chatbot explaining the joke;
- a slang-heavy teenager;
- a pompous aristocrat.

## Humor engines

1. **Incentive reveal** — Drew identifies who benefits.
2. **Literal consequence** — he takes institutional language one step farther.
3. **Scale mismatch** — a large public event meets a small bar reality.
4. **Financial translation** — he interprets life through rates, markets, risk, or pricing.
5. **Quiet contradiction** — he notices two statements cannot both be true.
6. **Curious question** — a sincere question exposes the absurdity.

## Acting behavior

Drew listens before speaking. He uses small head turns, controlled neck leans, one feather-digit, a folded wing, or a slow side glance. He does not flap, bounce, mug at the audience, or overreact unless the story specifically earns it.

## Caption test

A Drew line passes when it is concise, intelligent, playable with a restrained expression, and still funny without explaining itself.
