# Scene and Continuity Rules

## Scene assembly order

Build every scene in this order:

1. lock Drew's identity from the master model;
2. choose one approved expression and pose;
3. add scene-specific clothing if required;
4. add the held prop and interaction;
5. construct the environment;
6. add caption lettering or reserve clean caption space;
7. apply line, wash, and visual hierarchy;
8. run quality control.

The environment is never allowed to redesign the character.

## Default bar environment

When the setting is The Swinging Door:

- unmistakably American bar interior;
- heavy wood paneling and substantial wooden bar;
- beer taps and appropriate bottles when the scene requires them;
- visible daily-specials chalkboard when composition allows;
- recognizable swinging saloon-style door or established venue cue;
- minimal wall clutter;
- character and joke remain dominant over décor.

Specific scene instructions override optional background items. A one-off request to remove bottles does not permanently remove them from the venue bible.

## Camera continuity

Drew may appear front, three-quarter, profile, back three-quarter, back, seated, close, medium, or wide. The neck attaches consistently, beak bends the same direction relative to the skull, wing layering follows perspective, and the bow tie sits at the base of the neck.

## Prop continuity

- Signature martini: exactly three olives on one pick.
- Prop scale must suit the feather-hand grip.
- A prop appears only when named or narratively necessary.
- Objects do not fuse with feathers, beak, or body.
- Do not carry a prop from a previous image unless continuity requires it.

## Caption staging

Reserve clean negative space for captions and speech balloons. Keep tails unambiguous. Do not cover Drew's eyes, beak, bow tie, wing gesture, or the visual fact required for the joke.

## Background restraint

Render background detail one step lighter and looser than Drew. Deep contrast belongs on eyes, beak tip, bow tie, and essential props. Avoid over-rendered décor that competes with the characters.
